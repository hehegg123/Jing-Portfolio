Designing Transparent In-Vehicle Intelligent Agents

2021–2022 | Virginia Tech | Published Research

Overview

How should an automated vehicle explain its decisions to the driver?

We studied whether drivers respond differently when an in-vehicle agent explains its actions upfront or waits for a request. We also tested how the accuracy of those explanations shapes trust, workload, situation awareness, and takeover performance.

Background & Research Questions

When automation is in control, drivers may have little insight into why a vehicle slows down or changes lanes. Voice explanations can make those actions easier to understand. Their timing and accuracy may shape the driver’s experience.

  • How does proactive versus on-demand information delivery affect driver experience and performance?
  • How does information accuracy influence driver trust, workload, and situation awareness?
  • Does the effect of an information delivery strategy depend on reliability?

Research Approach

In a driving simulator, 25 participants experienced both voice strategies. One group received entirely accurate information, while the other received accurate information 60% of the time.

We collected driving performance measures, situation awareness assessments, standardized questionnaires, and user preferences.

Key Insights

  • Participants rated Push as more intelligent and competent. They rated Pull as warmer.
  • Participants reported higher physical demand with Pull. The extra step of requesting information may contribute to this difference.
  • Information delivery and reliability jointly affected peak lateral acceleration during takeover.
How information reliability affected driver awareness and effort
Mean score High reliability Low reliability
Awareness of surroundings (SAGAT Level 1) 81% 64%
Reported effort (NASA-TLX) 26.4 41.0

Both differences were statistically significant. Trust ratings showed no statistically significant difference by reliability.

UX Design Implications

  • Reduce interaction effort. Offer timely context when drivers have limited attention to ask for it.
  • Design for reliability. Account for the quality of available information when deciding what the agent should explain.
  • Preserve user control. Make additional detail easy to request when drivers have time and need more context.
